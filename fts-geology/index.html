FT’s Geology

The most realistic geology mod for Minecraft

Plates that drift, faults that produce the landforms they should, earthquakes you can watch happen, and volcanoes shaped by the ground they stand on.

Download What it builds
// THE MODEL

One Map,
Everything Else

Your world is divided into plates that drift. Where two of them meet, what happens depends on how they are moving relative to each other, exactly as it does on Earth. Nothing in this mod is decoration. Every volcano, every hot spring, every earthquake is downstream of that one fact.

Four Kinds,
Four Worlds

The Himalaya has the thickest crust on Earth and not one volcano. Tibet is covered in hot springs anyway. The model knows the difference.

The crust is pulled apart, so a whole block of ground drops between two faults. A graben valley with a fissure torn down its axis, fissure volcanism, geysers. Iceland.

Below: a swarm of vertical basalt dykes

One plate goes under the other. A trench is dug hard against the boundary while the overriding side is heaved up inland, where the stratovolcanoes stand.

Below: the slab, its magma chambers, granite roots

Neither plate will sink, so the crust crumples: one asymmetric range with a steep front, a broad plateau behind it, and a foreland basin at its foot. Hot springs, and no volcanoes.

Below: a thick root of folded marble and gneiss

Neither side rises or falls. One slides along the other and carries the landscape with it, so a stream bed crossing the fault is genuinely cut and offset. The San Andreas.

Below: a narrow scar of shattered rock

// EARTHQUAKES

Two Minutes
Of Ground

A rupture follows the real curve of the fault. Its length comes from the standard surface-rupture scaling law, its magnitude from the Gutenberg-Richter distribution. The whole boundary moves at once, slowly, over a couple of minutes. Plates do not unzip from a point; they pull apart everywhere along the line together.

Afterwards the ground settles. Spikes fall, crests shed talus at their feet, and the forest reseats itself: a tree whose ground dropped a little comes down with it, roots and all. Where the slope actually failed, the scarp is left bare.

Mountains
With Reasons

In the real world you do not get to choose the shape of a volcano. Thick, sticky magma above a subduction zone piles into a steep layered cone. Runny magma from a hotspot spreads into a vast gentle shield, or blows its roof off and leaves a caldera. You can tell where you are standing by the mountain.

The colours are alive

Each band around a hot spring is a community of microorganisms that can only survive inside its own temperature range. The ring you are standing on tells you how hot the water was when it got there. It is a thermometer you can see.

Above roughly 73 °C nothing survives, which is why the lip of the pool is bare silica sinter. Sulfur crusts the acidic fumaroles instead, so the ground also tells you the chemistry.

Sinter 73-93 °C
Orange mat 65-73 °C
Yellow mat 55-65 °C
Brown mat 45-55 °C
Green mat 30-45 °C

// GET IT

Alpha
0.9.0

It reads your world instead of replacing it

No worldgen is overridden, no biome source is taken over, no noise settings are touched. It stacks on top of Terralith and Tectonic rather than fighting them, and if a terrain mod has already painted a Yellowstone, the model settles on it.

Player-placed blocks are never broken. Not by generation, not by an eruption, not by an earthquake. Safe to add to a world you have already built in.
